Where to dump hazardous waste, tires and e-waste in Washington, DC

Washington, DC has 4 official sites listed for paint, electronics, tires, batteries, motor oil and household chemicals, including Fort Totten Transfer Station (tires). Source: dpw.dc.gov.

Updated November 25, 2026. Checked Nov 2026. Confirm with the official source before you go.

Inside DC there is no permanent HHW or e-cycling drop-off: Fort Totten takes up to 4 tires during regular hours, and HHW and electronics go to registered DPW Special Waste Collection Events. Source: https://dpw.dc.gov/service/household-hazardous-waste-hhw-e-cycling-document-shredding. Fairfax County, Virginia residents can use the county's permanent HHW and e-waste drop-offs at the I-66 Transfer Station and I-95 Landfill Complex (Fairfax County residents only).

Are the hours and fees guaranteed?

No. TossMap shows source links and last-checked dates so you can see where a listing came from and when it was reviewed. Hours, fees and accepted materials can change after that check. Confirm with the linked facility or government page before loading your vehicle and making the trip.

Can businesses use these drop-off sites?

Do not assume so. Many household hazardous-waste programs restrict drop-offs to residents and exclude commercial waste. Read the eligibility notes for the individual facility, then confirm with the linked government or operator source. A place listed near your business may still require a commercial disposal service or a separate appointment.
